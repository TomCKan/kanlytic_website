What Is Market Intelligence?

Market intelligence is the process of collecting and analysing information about the environment in which your business operates.

For accounting firms, this can include understanding:
• Competing accountancy practices
• Local business populations
• Industry trends
• Geographic opportunities
• Business formation rates
• Areas experiencing economic growth

Rather than relying on assumptions, market intelligence helps firms make evidence-based decisions.

Why Competitor Intelligence Matters

Most firms can name a handful of competitors, but many underestimate the number of practices operating within their wider catchment area.

Without understanding your competitive landscape, it becomes difficult to answer important strategic questions.

For example:
• Is our local market becoming more competitive?
• Are there neighbouring areas with fewer accounting firms?
• Which sectors appear underserved?
• Should we invest in another office?
• Are we targeting the right geographic areas?


Competitor intelligence provides a clearer picture of the market in which your firm operates.

Beyond Competitors: Understanding Opportunity

Good market intelligence is also about understanding demand.

Questions worth asking include:
• Where are new businesses being created?
• Which industries are growing?
• Which regions are seeing increased commercial activity?
• Where are businesses undergoing significant change?

When competitor information is combined with business activity, firms gain a much clearer understanding of both competition and opportunity.

Common Sources of Market Intelligence

Accounting firms typically gather information from multiple sources.

These might include:
• Companies House
• Office for National Statistics
• Google Maps
• Local business directories
• Industry reports
• Networking
• Local knowledge

Each source provides useful information, but collecting and combining it manually can take considerable time.


Why Manual Research Becomes Difficult

As firms grow, market research often becomes fragmented. Different partners maintain different spreadsheets, marketing teams use one source, business development teams use another.

By the time information has been collected, organised and reviewed, parts of it may already be outdated.

Having a consistent monthly source of market intelligence helps ensure decisions are based on current information rather than assumptions or anecdotal evidence.

Who Uses Market Intelligence?

Market intelligence supports a wide range of roles within accounting firms.

Managing Partners use it to inform strategic planning.
Business Development Managers use it to identify new markets and prioritise campaigns.
Marketing teams use it to plan sector-focused activity.

What is competitor intelligence?
Competitor intelligence involves collecting and analysing information about competing businesses to support better commercial decisions.
Is competitor intelligence only useful for large firms?
No. Smaller firms often benefit just as much by understanding local competition before investing in marketing or expansion.
How often should market intelligence be updated?
Regular updates help firms respond to changing market conditions. For most strategic planning and business development activities, monthly updates provide a practical balance between timeliness and usability.
Can public data provide useful market intelligence?
Yes. Public sources such as Companies House contain valuable information, but analysing and organising that data into meaningful insights often requires significant time and expertise.
